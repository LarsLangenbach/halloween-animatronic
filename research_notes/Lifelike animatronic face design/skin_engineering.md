# Skin engineering for a lifelike animatronic face (material, thickness, anchoring, sliding, folds)

Scope: how professional and research animatronic/android face skins are specified, made and attached so they crease, fold and bulge like faces instead of stretching like a rubber mask. Written for a hobby build: brushed Smooth-On Dragon Skin FX-Pro in a plaster mold off a Monster Clay sculpt, a 3D-printed skull, silicone snap pads, Will Cogley's mouth mechanism and EyeMech, two MG90S brow mechanisms, no drill. Research date 2026-10-30.

Unit conversions used throughout (mine, not the sources'): 1 psi = 0.006895 MPa; 1 pli (pounds per linear inch, tear) = 0.1751 N/mm; 1 in = 25.4 mm.

Project context checked in the repo: `cad/params.py` currently assumes `SKIN_T = 5.0` mm silicone over the skull and `LIP_SKIN_T = 8.0` mm in front of the Cogley lip plates (both tagged ASSUMPTION), and brow snap pads `BROW_PAD_T = 3.0` mm.

## 1. Material choice for moving faces

### Takeaway
For an animatronic face the pros' rule is "as soft and low-modulus as you can get while still surviving tear at the anchors." Dragon Skin FX-Pro (Shore 2A, 763 % elongation, 61 pli tear) sits between Ecoflex 00-30 (softer, weaker) and Dragon Skin 10 (stiffer, much tougher); it is a sound choice, but its published 100 % modulus looks inconsistent and should be measured on a test strip before sizing servo loads. Body Double is a life-casting rubber (25A) and is too hard for a moving skin; foam latex and Frubber need equipment a hobbyist lacks.

- Disney's Physical Face Cloning work used GI-245, a two-part silicone from Silicones, Inc., with stiffness tuned by plasticizer concentration (no percentages in the visible text), modelled as compressible neo-Hookean; the model matched ISO 37 tests from −20 % to +50 % strain — [US9082222B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9082222)
- Ecoflex shows stress softening (Mullins effect) after the first load cycle, which "could recover significantly with time" — [Swansea University cronfa57203](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa57203)

### Inferences
- **The FX-Pro 100 % modulus looks wrong or at least surprising.** Smooth-On lists 37.8 psi for a 2A rubber, higher than Dragon Skin 10's 22 psi at 10A. If the number is right, FX-Pro is about 3.8× stiffer in stretch than Ecoflex 00-30 (10 psi) despite feeling soft to the touch. Measure it: cast a 100 × 20 × 5 mm strip, hang a known mass, measure extension. Neo-Hookean back-of-envelope: E ≈ σ100 / 0.58, so FX-Pro E ≈ 0.45 MPa (as listed) vs Ecoflex 00-30 E ≈ 0.12 MPa.
- **Rough pull force on a skin band** (neo-Hookean, my arithmetic, crude because a real face pulls a 2D region, not a strip): a 20 mm wide × 5 mm thick band stretched 20 % needs about 7.5 N in FX-Pro (as listed) vs about 2 N in Ecoflex 00-30. Force scales linearly with thickness, so 3 mm instead of 5 mm cuts it by 40 %. Compare with servo force F = torque / horn radius before committing to a thickness.
- **Why FX-Pro is still a good call for this build:** its tear strength (61 pli) is 1.6× Ecoflex 00-30 and 2.8× Ecoflex 00-10, and anchors and mouth corners fail by tear (see Q6). It brushes as a thick 18,000 cps liquid with a 12 min pot life, suits Psycho Paint, and is Smooth-On's appliance-skin product. The trade is a stiffer stretch.
- **Hybrid option that stays within one product family:** neat FX-Pro for the first (outer, detail) brush coats and for anchor zones; FX-Pro + 50 parts Slacker per 100B (about 00-20, slight tack) for the bulk coats in zones that should bulge and fold (cheeks, nasolabial region, under-eye). Platinum silicones bond to each other when the next coat goes on before the previous one has fully cured (Disney's US7740953 relies on the same chemical bonding between pours). Test tear at that ratio: Smooth-On publishes no tear data with Slacker.
- **Avoid Silicone Thinner as a softener**: it trades tear strength away in proportion and is capped at 10 %. Slacker is the softener; Thinner is only for flow.
- **Professionals vs static prosthetics:** static prosthetics are encapsulated, deadened, gel-filled and glued to a person; animatronic skins are not encapsulated (Ellerby), are mechanically anchored, and use a hardness ladder (soft fat/backing, soft visible skin, hard drive inserts). Foam latex (light, low force) and foamed elastomers (Frubber) are the low-force choices but need ovens, injection or proprietary foaming, so they are out of reach here.

### Gaps
- No fatigue-to-failure cycle counts found for Dragon Skin FX-Pro, Ecoflex or PlatSil under facial-strain cycling.
- No tear or elongation data for Smooth-On silicones mixed with Slacker.
- PlatSil Gel-00 / Gel-OO30 data not retrieved (the brief named them); only Gel-10 found.
- Hanson's Frubber force/power figures come from press extracts of a page I could not open; the original Hanson paper was not found.
- Columbia's Emo skin material is not stated in the sources I could open.
- MG90S torque was not researched here; use the datasheet value in the F = T / r check.

## 2. Thickness mapping (lips, eyelids, brows, cheeks, nasolabial folds, forehead; hinge lines; fat layers)

### Takeaway
Thickness is the main design lever: Disney's face-cloning work optimised only the skin's inner surface (thickness) to make a face deform correctly, and its patents put the visible skin at about 3 mm (under 0.125 in) within a 2–10 mm range, with soft foam or gel "backing" behind it to make regions move as a unit. No professional source publishes a per-region millimetre map; region values have to be found by test skins.

### Cited Findings
- Disney's "Physical Face Cloning" (Bickel et al., SIGGRAPH 2012) fits skin shape and spatially varying thickness, plus actuation parameters, so the robot's skin matches captured target expressions — [Disney Research publication page](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/physical-face-cloning/); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2012-08-physical-cloning-method.html)
- In the patent, thickness is changed only through the inner surface, by a moving-least-squares warp in the thickness direction; a penalty term **prevents thickness from increasing** (only thinning is allowed); thickness is held constant near attachment links; attachment points are fixed during optimisation; only four expressive poses were used to optimise from over 100 captured frames — [US9082222B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9082222)
- Disney US7740953 (skin with elastic components of differing hardness): exterior skin "less than 0.25 inches and more typically less than about 0.125 inches" (under 6.4 mm, typically under 3.2 mm); behind it a "thick, soft layer" of silicone foam or silicone/urethane foam backing (Shore 00 about 1–25) so the face around a drive point "moves with the mouth" as a unit instead of only at the point — [US7740953B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7740953)
- Disney US20130280493 (subsurface reinforcement): robotic skin "2 to 10 millimeters or the like (0.1 to 0.5 inches or the like)"; two-pour skins with an inner layer of Shore A 10 or higher and a softer Shore 00 outer layer for feel — [US20130280493A1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130280493A1/en)
- Disney US10137616 (lightweight skins for large robots) cuts **grooves or channels into the inner surface** "to increase the flexibility or allow easier bending" in a chosen direction, as strain relief on pieces that flex — a direct analogue of a hinge line — [US10137616B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10137616)
- Disney US10118320 (biomimetic skin, two layers: a nonporous outer "dermis" over a thicker porous 3D-spacer-fabric "hypodermis"): lateral compression makes the outer layer crease; lateral stretch flattens creases; a point load thins the inner layer and dents the outer one; the inner layer's thickness limits lateral stretch; **relief cuts** in the inner layer pre-weaken it (a shoulder region "may be prefabricated to provide 20-40° greater flexibility"); overall depth is varied, thinner over prominent bone — [US10118320B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10118320)
- Hanson's patent casts the skin between an inner and an outer mold to control thickness "where desirable or throughout its form," and suggests using forensic tissue-depth data to set the inner form so thickness is roughly anatomical (no numbers given) — [US7113848B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- David Boccabella (Stan Winston School moderator): "Too thin and you will see the pressure points of the movement points underneath. Too thick and the servo's will not have the strength to move the skin." He sets thickness with a clay layer inside the outer mold before making the inner mold, and makes cheap gelatine test skins, piercing them with a needle to measure thickness and adjusting the underskull until it works — [Stan Winston School forum, underskull thread](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/8954/)
- Jeffrey Warren Park: build the skin in sections, soft where there is heavy movement and firmer where there is little; worn sections can then be replaced — [Stan Winston School forum, "Do I need to encapsulate silicone?"](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524)
- InMoov's i2 head (Gaël Langevin) uses a thin silicone mask poured in a 3D-printed two-part mold; the eyelids are glued "because they're so thin," the rest is held with Velcro and some magnets — [Make: "Skin Deep – Evolving InMoov's Facial Expressions With AI"](https://makezine.com/article/technology/robotics/skin-deep-evolving-inmoovs-facial-expressions-with-ai)
- Slacker-softened Dragon Skin FX-Pro is Smooth-On's recommended gel fill for "encapsulated" (gel-filled) appliances; Slacker gels also serve as cushioning in anaplastology — [Smooth-On Slacker](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/slacker/)
- Chris Ellerby suggests limiting silicone thickness to keep weight down and building thick areas up with polyfoam — [Stan Winston School forum, "Foam Latex VS Silicone Skin"](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/21185/)

### Inferences
- **Mechanics behind hinge lines (standard plate theory, not from a source above):** bending stiffness of a sheet scales with thickness cubed (D = E t³ / 12(1 − ν²)), stretch stiffness only linearly. Halving the thickness along a line makes it 8× easier to bend there but only 2× easier to stretch. That is why a thin groove on the back of the skin decides where the fold forms, and why a uniformly thick skin "stretches like rubber" instead of folding.
- **Suggested starting map for brushed FX-Pro** (my synthesis of Disney's ~3 mm visible skin, the 2–10 mm range, the project's 5 mm assumption, and the "thin only" rule; validate with test skins):
  - Forehead: 3–4 mm, with 1–1.5 mm-deep shallow grooves on the inner side under each horizontal forehead crease you sculpt, so the brows raise wrinkles in the sculpted places.
  - Brows: 4–6 mm (they carry the snap pads and should move as a block; locally thicker or backed by a firmer insert).
  - Upper and lower eyelids: as thin as the process allows, about 1–2 mm, glued to the moving lid shells (InMoov glues its lids for this reason).
  - Under-eye / crow's feet: thin, 2–3 mm, with inner relief grooves radiating from the outer canthus.
  - Cheeks: the visible skin 3 mm plus a soft Slacker-gel or foam "fat" layer behind it where a smile should bulge, with the skin free (not glued) over that area.
  - Nasolabial fold: a thin line (1.5–2 mm) on the inner surface along the sculpted fold, bounded by thicker cheek "fat" above and an anchor below, so the cheek piles onto it when the mouth corner is pulled.
  - Lips: thick and soft (the project already assumes 8 mm) with a harder insert or fabric anchor at the corners (see Q3).
- **Making a variable thickness with brushed silicone and no inner mold:** count brush coats per zone (mask a zone with tape or a clay dam before later coats), add THI-VEX for build-up coats, and brush the thin hinge lines with fewer coats. Or apply Disney's "thin only" rule in clay: sculpt the inner surface of a core or the clay thickness layer thick and carve thin channels where folds should form.
- **Gelatine test skins** (Boccabella) are a cheap way to tune a region before spending FX-Pro.

### Gaps
- No professional source found that publishes millimetre thicknesses per facial region (lips, eyelids, brows, cheeks, nasolabial, forehead); the Physical Face Cloning paper's resulting thickness range was not in the text I could read.
- No source found with numbers for groove depth or width that makes a fold form at a chosen line in silicone of a given thickness.

## 3. Anchoring (pull points, fabric/power mesh, rigid inserts, magnets, snap pads, glued vs free zones)

### Takeaway
Every serious source converges on the same rule: never pull silicone at a point. Spread each actuator's force through a cast-in pad (cloth, mesh or a firmer elastomer with tapered wings), give the drive a hard socket inside that pad, lock the skin to the skull at the places that must not move (mouth corners, along fold lines), and leave the bulging regions free. Where you anchor decides where the folds form.

### Cited Findings
- Hanson US 7,113,848: flexible anchors of "rubber or cloth" are embedded in the skin to distribute force through a region, prevent tearing and improve appearance; mouth-corner anchors may include a **semi-rigid crescent along the smile crease**, or a cloth appendage that forms a dimple; anchors can extend into the lips; eyelids get curved rigid or semi-rigid bands embedded in the lid skin; anchors are pinned into the face-form mold before casting so they cure into the skin, or glued in afterwards; linkages attach by sewing, gluing, or a barbed hook; the skull-like frame lets the skin "ride over the frame"; a sliding affector runs through a slot in the frame (with a flange so it cannot pull through); actuators named are servos (main embodiment), SMA or EAP; linkages cable, thread, braided nylon, Kevlar, carbon fibre or rod — [US7113848B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- Hanson has described the cast-in anchors connected to small motors as simulating facial connective tissue — [EarthSky, "David Hanson creates robots with human faces"](https://earthsky.org/human-world/david-hanson-creates-robots-with-human-faces/)
- Disney US8568642 (integral actuation points, "EAPs"): point attachments look wrong because "the forces applied by the robotics actuators or drivers are applied as point forces," so movement is "undesirably localized... only at the point of attachment," and glued-on connectors detach or tear under repeated motion. EAPs are seated on ball-tipped posts (less than about 0.25 in proud of the mold core) before the pour and bond in during cure; EAP body 30–50 durometer, bearing member 50–60 durometer or a plastic, ceramic or metal insert; skin softer than the EAP so it stretches around it; EAP thickness "3/4 down to 1/4 of the thickness of the skin (e.g., 1/8 to 1/4 inches)"; actuators end in a ball that snaps into the EAP socket with "tactile and audible feedback"; separate **locking EAPs** fix the skin to the shell and **force-distributing EAPs** take actuator load; tapered wings spread loads and EAP shapes "block or end tear paths previously found at valleys or sharp corners of the skin"; the mouth corner is a common crack site and gets locking EAPs; FDM-printed parts are acceptable for inner skin components — [US8568642B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8568642)
- Disney US7740953: a harder force-receiving insert at the mouth takes the jaw drive pins; a very soft backing wraps it so the surrounding face moves with the mouth; inserts glued to the backing with RTV silicone sealant (Dow Corning 732 in the example); the exterior skin is poured last and bonds chemically to backing and insert, with recesses in the inner parts for grip; multiple anchor points "at or near eyebrows" simulate separate muscles; hardness ladder backing < skin < insert (tune by test) — [US7740953B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7740953)
- Disney US20130280493: a four-way-stretch spandex (elastane) mesh sock is embedded **inside** the skin at typically 1/16–3/16 in (1.6–4.8 mm) depth, at least about 1/16 in, so it is bonded on both faces and "less likely to pull out," and the drive elements bear on rubber, not fabric; depth can vary across one skin (deeper in high-wear areas); conical standoffs on the core hold the mesh at depth and cause "less 'print through'" — [US20130280493A1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130280493A1/en)
- Other Disney skin patents in this family (not read in full): US10913161 "Robotics skin system with integral elastomeric links to complete mechanical linkages," US8927081 "Flexible seam joint for use in robotic skin," US7887729 "Fabricating multi-component skin systems" — [US10913161B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10913161); [US8927081B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8927081); [Justia US7887729](https://patents.justia.com/patent/7887729)
- Stan Winston School forum: admin Chris Ellerby attaches control cables to "small fabric pads that glue to your silicone skin with silicone adhesive" ("you can still peel the pads off"), and holds the skin to the underskull with silicone adhesive he can "just peel up when needed"; he warned that embedded neodymium magnets "may put a lot of stress on the skin" and "will start to discolor the surface" by attracting metal particles; the original poster noted lip magnets could "snap to each other instead of the control cable"; a threaded stud with fabric backing was suggested but untried — [Stan Winston School forum, "Attaching control cables to silicone mask"](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/13465/)
- Chris Ellerby: reinforce with "power mesh" 2-way stretch fabric at the mouth and around the neck where the skin stretches over the underskull — [Stan Winston School forum, "Do I need to encapsulate silicone?"](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524); David Boccabella: nylon stocking material reinforces the lips and mouth edge — [Stan Winston School forum, underskull thread](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/8954/)

- Columbia's Emo (Hu et al., Science Robotics, March 2024, DOI 10.1126/scirobotics.adi4724): a human-like head with 26 actuators under a soft silicone skin "with a magnetic attachment system, allowing for easy customization and quick maintenance" — [ScienceDaily, 2024-03-27](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/03/240327154854.htm); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1039361)
- University of Tokyo (Kawai, Nie, Oda, Takeuchi, Cell Reports Physical Science, June 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.xcrp.2024.102066): "perforation-type anchors" inspired by skin ligaments — the skin material fills **V-shaped holes through the underlying structure**, so anchors are flush, unlike earlier hooks or mini anchors that limited surfaces and could damage the skin in motion; motor force transmitted through these anchors made a face smile — [University of Tokyo press release](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/press/z0508_00360.html); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/06/240625205041.htm)
- Sil-Poxy (Smooth-On silicone adhesive) bonds RTV silicones to silicone, fabrics, "some plastics (urethane)," plaster; pot life 5 min, handling 1 h, full cure 24 h, Shore 40A, 750 psi (5.2 MPa) tensile, 750 % elongation; prepare by abrading and solvent-wiping; no claim for 3D-printed plastics (test first); the page lists prosthetics and animatronics among uses — [Smooth-On Sil-Poxy](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/sil-poxy/)
- Disney's biomimetic skin anchors by passing fasteners or integral frame hooks through apertures in the porous inner layer, or with adhesive, welding or hook-and-loop — [US10118320B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10118320)

### Inferences
- **Mapping the pro pattern onto this build's snap pads:** each snap pad is a Disney "EAP." Make each one a firmer core (neat FX-Pro is too soft; a Dragon Skin 10/20 pour or Sil-Poxy at 40A, or a printed plastic insert) with thin tapered wings, cast or brushed into the skin while the skin's inner coats are still tacky, with a disc of power mesh bridging pad and skin 1.6–4.8 mm below the outer surface. The mechanism side ends in a printed ball or mushroom stud that the pad's socket snaps over. This matches the project's "horns bear on printed stubs; screws only retain" rule.
- **Anchor placement controls folds:** a fold forms where moving skin is pushed against skin that cannot move. For a Frankenstein smile or snarl: lock the skin at the nasolabial line (a locking pad or a glued strip on the skull along the fold), pull the mouth corner up/back with a force pad, and leave the cheek free and padded so it bulges above the locked line. Locking pads at the mouth corners also stop the classic corner tear.
- **Brows:** two pads per brow (inner and outer, as `BROW_PADS_X` already assumes) let the brow tilt; glue or lock a horizontal strip across the upper forehead so raising the brow compresses the forehead skin between brow and strip, which is what makes horizontal wrinkles appear where the sculpt and inner grooves put them.
- **No-drill anchors:** the Tokyo perforation anchor is printable. Print V-shaped or dovetailed through-holes (or undercut pockets) in the skull at the locking zones and let brushed silicone flow into them while making a bonded pad, or print a small plate with perforations that is cast into the skin. Silicone does not bond to PLA/PETG, so a mechanical key is the reliable way to "glue" without a primer.
- **Magnets:** fine for quick-release locking points away from each other (Cogley, Emo, InMoov), but keep them out of the lips and moving pairs, embed them behind enough silicone that they do not stress or discolour the surface, and expect them to slip under shear unless they sit in a cup.
- **Glued zones:** silicone adhesive (Sil-Poxy, or a silicone sealant like the Dow Corning 732 in Disney's patent) for silicone-to-silicone and silicone-to-fabric; use mechanical keys for silicone-to-printed-plastic.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation found from Legacy Effects, Jim Henson's Creature Shop, Spectral Motion or Garner Holt on how they attach skins; only Disney/WDI patents, Hanson's patent, and Stan Winston School forum staff.
- Emo's magnet count, size and placement were not in any source I could open.
- No numbers on pull-out force of fabric pads or snap pads from silicone.

## 4. Sliding: letting skin move over the skull

### Takeaway
No authoritative source specifies a lubricant for skin over an underskull. What the evidence supports: friction and changing skin-to-shell contact cause hysteresis (the face lags and does not return to rest the same way), soft silicone gets tacky, a barrier coat or dusting reduces grab, and the skull should be a smooth, skull-shaped surface the skin can ride over, with reinforcement where the skin stretches over it.

### Cited Findings
- Affetto (Osaka): the authors recommend attaching skin to the shell "smoothly and elastically," because hysteresis grows when skin-shell contact changes in a hysteretic way; hysteresis (nβ) "was larger than 50 for every DU except DUs 1 and 12" and realised motion lagged ideal motion mainly because of hysteresis — [Ishihara, Wu & Asada 2018, Frontiers in Robotics and AI](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00119/full)
- Skin deformation in an android face comes from interactions of soft skin, skull-shaped shell, transmission lines and actuators, which the authors say makes measuring the assembled face necessary — [Ishihara, Iwanaga & Asada 2021, Frontiers in Robotics and AI (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019968)
- Hanson's frame is skull-like so the skin can "ride over the frame"; a sliding affector runs in a frame slot — [US7113848B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- Softer silicone is more likely to be sticky; a barrier coat makes handling and attaching easier; "The less resistance the skin gives to the mechanics' movement, the easier the whole animatronic will work" — [Stan Winston School forum, comment 11519](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11519); [comment 11524](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524)
- Slacker raises tack with dose (FX-Pro: slight at 50 parts, very tacky at 150–200) — [Smooth-On Slacker](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/slacker/)
- A forum commenter reported a specialty powder cut silicone tack better and longer than talc (search extract) — [Stan Winston School forum, comment 13504](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/13504)
- A 1977 cosmetic-science study found talcum powder and silicone oil both reduce skin friction, but only when applied in sufficient amounts — [In Vivo Skin Friction Measurements (SCC library)](https://library.scconline.org/cdn-1708702563092/In-Vivo-Skin-Friction-Measurements.pdf)
- Flocking "does work on silicone" but "does not like to be handled a lot, so over time you will lose fibers" (Chris Ellerby) — [Stan Winston School forum, "Foam Latex VS Silicone Skin"](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/21185/)
- Power mesh is recommended around the neck "where it stretches to slide over your underskull" — [Stan Winston School forum, comment 11524](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524)
- Embedding spandex mesh lets drive elements bear on rubber and makes the skin more flexible at the reinforced joint areas (neck, elbows) — [US20130280493A1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130280493A1/en)

### Inferences
- **Treat the skull as a bearing surface where skin must slide** (cheeks, forehead under the brows, around the mouth): sand or coat the printed skull smooth there (layer lines act like a rasp on silicone), keep a small clearance (about 0.5–1 mm) in sliding zones so the skin is not pre-loaded against the shell, and keep the inner skin surface neat FX-Pro (not Slacker-tacky) in those zones.
- **Lubricant choices to test** (no source ranks them for this use): unscented talc or a dedicated silicone de-tack powder on the inner skin surface; a thin silicone-oil film on the skull (it also slightly swells some silicones; test on an offcut); a stretch-fabric (Lycra/power mesh) liner bonded to the inner skin face in sliding zones, which gives a non-tacky, low-friction face and reinforcement at once.
- **Servo-load implication:** friction adds load that does not return the skin to rest; the face then depends on the skin's own elasticity to come back, which shows up as Affetto-style hysteresis. Measure it: drive a brow to its limit and back with the skin on, and compare rest positions.

### Gaps
- No measured friction coefficients found for platinum silicone on PLA/PETG, dry, powdered or oiled.
- No professional shop documentation on underskull lubrication or flocking for animatronic faces.

## 5. Computational and research methods (Disney, Osaka Affetto, Columbia Eva/Emo, Hanson, Tokyo) and what a hobbyist can copy

### Takeaway
The research is mostly about measuring and then shaping: Disney optimised skin thickness and attachment paths in simulation; Osaka measured how each actuator moves the whole face and found androids lack the curved flow lines and undulation of human faces; Columbia learned its face's mapping by "self-modelling" in front of a camera. A hobbyist can copy the loop by hand: mark dots, film each actuator alone, compare against a human reference, and thin or re-anchor skin where the motion is wrong.

### Cited Findings
- **Disney Physical Face Cloning (2012):** pipeline = measure the elastic properties of the skin material → capture 3D target expressions of a real person → simulate the skin on the robot under actuation → optimise skin shape (thickness) and actuation parameters to best match the targets → fabricate; output-oriented (models the synthetic skin, does not copy real tissue) — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2012-08-physical-cloning-method.html); [Disney Research](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/physical-face-cloning/)
- Material calibration used 55 × 5 × 2 mm stretch strips and 50 mm × 12 mm compression cylinders; 95 mm test bars came out within 0.44 mm (top) and 0.38 mm (bottom) average error of the goal shape; each attachment link's motion was mapped by tracking a marker while sampling actuator values and interpolating; skins were liquid-injection-molded with a pressure gun into **3D-printed molds** (a core plus six outer parts) and cured at room temperature for about seven days before use; 27k-tet simulation mesh, 64k in a refined area — [US9082222B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9082222)
- Press coverage notes the team's planned future work included a more flexible, multi-layered skin — [ISPR, 2012-08-20](https://ispr.info/2012/08/20/disney-develops-face-cloning-technique-for-animatronics)
- Disney patent US9403099 covers modelling skin-covered robotics with artistic digital iterative design (not read) — [Justia US9403099](https://patents.justia.com/patent/9403099)
- **Osaka Affetto (2018):** treated the face as a system with actuator commands in and surface displacement fields out; measured 3D displacements of 116 surface markers with each of 16 drive units actuated alone (22 pneumatic DoFs total); the largest displacement is near each connection point, but motion propagates widely (markers far from the peak moved "over 1 mm"), displacement vectors point in varied directions so a connection point plus a nominal direction does not describe an actuator; lessons: measure each actuator's displacement field, improve skin-to-shell attachment to cut hysteresis, add feedback, and use inverse response functions to design command sequences — [Ishihara, Wu & Asada 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00119/full)
- **Osaka flow lines (2021):** android faces (an adult female android and Affetto) lack two upper-face features found in adult men: curved flow lines and surface undulation; flow lines around the eyes and forehead were nearly straight in androids; the authors argue "composite motion mechanisms" controlling both are needed — [Ishihara, Iwanaga & Asada 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019968); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/685139)
- **Columbia Emo (2024):** 26 actuators under magnetically attached soft silicone skin; trained by "self-modeling" (random movements in front of a camera to learn the link between motor commands and expressions), then by watching human videos; predicts a smile about 840 ms ahead — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/03/240327154854.htm)
- **Hanson:** anchors cast into foamed elastomer distribute actuator force through skin regions; mouth-corner crescents and dimple appendages shape specific creases — [US7113848B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- **Tokyo (2024):** flush, ligament-like perforation anchors transmit actuator force into the skin without protruding hooks — [University of Tokyo](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/en/press/z0508_00360.html)

### Inferences
- **Hobbyist version of Disney + Affetto, no software needed:**
  1. Calibrate the material: one 100 × 20 × 5 mm FX-Pro strip, hang weights, record extension (gives E; check the 37.8 psi claim).
  2. Draw a grid of dots (e.g. 10 mm spacing) on the test skin with a silicone-safe marker.
  3. Film each servo alone at its limits from the front and side; then a human (or the reference images) making the same action.
  4. Where the robot's dots move too little far from an anchor, the skin is too stiff or the anchor too small: enlarge the pad or thin the skin there. Where motion is concentrated in a dimple at the anchor, add a backing/fat layer or a larger tapered pad. Where folds form in the wrong place, add a thin line (inner groove) where you want them and a locking pad on the other side.
  5. Remember Disney's rule: change thickness by thinning, keep it fixed at anchors.
- **Osaka's flow-line finding** suggests pulling forehead and eye-region skin along curved paths, not straight lines: e.g. angle the brow pads' travel, or use two pads per brow at different heights so the arc bends.
- The Columbia self-modelling idea can be used without AI: record servo positions against tracked dot positions to build a lookup table for each pose, and use it for firmware soft limits.

### Gaps
- No later Disney Research paper (2013–2026) specifically on animatronic face-skin attachment optimisation was found in my searches; only the 2012 work and patents.
- Columbia Eva (2021) skin details not retrieved.
- The full Physical Face Cloning paper PDF did not load, so the resulting thickness range and attachment count are unknown.

## 6. Failure modes and how to test and fix them

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are tears starting at point anchors and mouth corners, glued connectors letting go, wrong-place or no wrinkling from uniform thickness, servo stall from thick or stiff skin, tack and drag from over-softened silicone, and drift from stress softening and friction. Most are prevented by distributed anchors, a hardness ladder, and test skins.

### Cited Findings
- **Tear at anchors and corners:** glued-on connectors detach or tear under repeated motion; tear paths start at valleys or sharp corners; the mouth corner is a common crack site; fix with locking pads and tapered wings that end tear paths — [US8568642B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8568642)
- **Hanson** embeds rubber or cloth anchors specifically to "prevent tearing" and improve appearance — [US7113848B2, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- **Mesh pull-out:** fabric on the inner surface pulls out; embedding it at ≥ 1/16 in depth, bonded both sides, makes it "less likely to pull out during use" — [US20130280493A1, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130280493A1/en)
- **Localised, mechanical-looking motion ("rubber mask" look):** point forces move skin only at the attachment point; a soft backing makes the region move as a unit; a softer exterior backed by a softer layer looks more natural — [US8568642B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8568642); [US7740953B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7740953)
- **Show-through:** anchor points tend to show when the skin is pulled from behind (Disney patent background text, seen in a search extract; the wording matches US10137616's background, which I read only in part); too-thin skin shows the "pressure points of the movement points underneath"; mesh standoff geometry affects print-through — [US10137616B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10137616); [Stan Winston School forum](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/8954/); [US20130280493A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130280493A1/en)
- **Servo stall:** "Too thick and the servo's will not have the strength to move the skin" — [Stan Winston School forum](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/8954/)
- **Delamination / adhesion:** silicone is "a pain to glue down"; Todd Debreceni's Pro Bond primer is suggested to make silicone adhere to nearly anything (a little goes a long way) — [Stan Winston School forum, comment 21185](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/21185/); [comment 11524](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524). Sil-Poxy needs abrading and solvent wiping and should be tested on unlisted plastics — [Smooth-On Sil-Poxy](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/sil-poxy/)
- **Magnet problems:** surface discolouration from attracted metal particles, skin stress, and lip magnets snapping together — [Stan Winston School forum, comment 13465](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/13465/)
- **Tack:** Slacker-softened silicone becomes tacky with dose; softer skins are stickier — [Smooth-On Slacker](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/slacker/); [Stan Winston School forum](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524)
- **Stress softening (Mullins effect)** in Ecoflex after the first cycle, partly recovering with time — [Swansea University cronfa57203](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa57203)
- **Hysteresis and drift** from changing skin-shell contact (Affetto) — [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frobt.2018.00119/full)
- **Thinner weakens:** Silicone Thinner cuts tear and tensile strength in proportion to dose — [Smooth-On Silicone Thinner](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/silicone-thinner/)
- **Repairability:** build skins in sections so worn areas can be replaced — [Stan Winston School forum](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524); tears in a layered skin stay in the top layer and are easier to repair — [US10118320B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10118320)
- **Test first:** "Make a few test skins," since forum advice is anecdotal (Jeffrey Warren Park); use gelatine test skins to tune thickness (Boccabella) — [comment 11524](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524); [comment 8954](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/8954/)

### Inferences
- **Test plan for this build** (each cheap, no drill):
  1. Strip test (E and tear start) for neat FX-Pro and FX-Pro + 50 Slacker.
  2. Pad pull-out: cast a snap pad with and without mesh into a 5 mm FX-Pro coupon, hang increasing weights until it pulls out or tears; record the mass. Compare against servo force at the horn radius used.
  3. Corner tear: a coupon with a 2 mm notch at the corner of a pad, cycled by hand or by a servo several hundred times.
  4. Brow cycle on the bench rig (`brow_rig.py`) with a skin patch on; note rest-position drift after 100 cycles (Mullins + friction).
  5. Pre-cycle the finished skin through every pose a few dozen times before setting firmware soft limits, because the first cycles soften the silicone.
- **"Rubber mask" fixes, in order of effort:** add a soft backing behind the moving region; enlarge or taper the force pad; add a locking line on the far side of the intended fold; thin the fold line; re-sculpt the fold in clay deeper so it starts folded.
- **Stall fix:** thin the skin over the moving region, move the anchor closer to the skin region that should move (shorter pulled span), or shorten the horn radius only if travel allows; never solve stall by adding Silicone Thinner.

### Gaps
- No numeric pull-out or tear-initiation forces at anchors found in any source.
- No cycle-life data for any of these silicones under facial-strain cycling.

## 7. Finishing for lifelikeness (intrinsic vs extrinsic paint, translucency, mouth interior, eyelid edges, seams)

### Takeaway
Silicone skins are coloured in two ways: intrinsically (Silc-Pig pigment in the brushed layers, giving depth and translucency) and extrinsically (Psycho Paint, a thinned platinum silicone paint airbrushed or stippled on, which stretches 1,000 % with the skin). Paint within two days of demolding, never on mold release, and keep the paint layers thin so they do not stiffen the folds.

### Cited Findings
- **Psycho Paint** is a two-part translucent platinum silicone paint base for FX, prosthetics and doll makers, applied in thin layers on cured platinum silicone (Dragon Skin, Ecoflex). Pre-mix about 2 % Silc-Pig by weight into Part B (do not exceed 5 % of total A+B), add equal Part A, then 200–500 parts solvent (NOVOCS Zero VOC; toluene, xylene or naphtha also work). Airbrush in thin layers or stipple with a brush. Surfaces must be clean and free of mold release (solvent-clean first). Best results when painting silicone no more than 2 days after demolding. Cured paint stretches and bends without cracking (1,000 % elongation), resists abrasion, is certified skin safe, cures in about 2 h (heat may speed it, max 100 °C, after the solvent has evaporated). Pot life is inconsistent on the page: 20 min undiluted in the instructions vs 45 min in the data table — [Smooth-On Psycho Paint](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/psycho-paint/)
- Dragon Skin FX-Pro is designed for painted finishes with the Psycho Paint system and takes Silc-Pig pigments in the bulk — [Smooth-On Dragon Skin FX-Pro](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/dragon-skin-fx-pro/)
- Slacker mixes can be pigmented by adding Silc-Pig to the pre-mix to match skin tones — [Smooth-On Slacker](https://www.smooth-on.com/products/slacker/)
- Silicone-only skins need silicone paints; with a New Baldies barrier coat most paints can be used — [Stan Winston School forum](https://forums.stanwinstonschool.com/discussion/comment/11524)
- Disney's biomimetic outer layer can be split into an outer sublayer for texture and an inner one for bulk — [US10118320B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10118320)
- Hanson forms eye sockets by placing eyeball shapes in the mold before casting; eyelid skin carries embedded curved bands — [US7113848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7113848B2/en)
- Thin eyelid skin is glued rather than Velcro'd or magnet-held (InMoov); Gaël Langevin noted that skin smoothness "makes quite a difference" and chose a non-human colour to avoid the uncanny valley — [Make:](https://makezine.com/article/technology/robotics/skin-deep-evolving-inmoovs-facial-expressions-with-ai)
- Will Cogley exaggerated proportions to make his head more cartoonish and reduce the uncanny-valley effect; reviewers still found the tone dull grey and zombie-like — [Arduino Blog](https://blog.arduino.cc/2024/12/09/this-frighteningly-realistic-animatronic-head-features-expressive-silicone-skin/); [TechEBlog](https://www.techeblog.com/homemade-animatronic-head-robot/)
- Disney's laser-cut skins can carry fine wrinkles and pores as surface detail; interlocking pieces are edge-matched for assembly — [US10137616B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10137616)
- Disney skin sections can be joined by adhesive, tongue-and-groove, mortise-and-tenon or dovetail joints — [US10118320B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10118320); Disney also holds a patent on a "flexible seam joint for use in robotic skin" (not read) — [US8927081B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8927081)

### Inferences
- **Brushed-layer colour order (intrinsic):** first brush coat into the mold = the outermost skin, so tint it with the surface tone (for Frankenstein, a grey-green) at low pigment load to keep translucency; deeper coats can carry a warmer or darker "flesh" tint, or the stitch-scar and vein colours, so they read through the outer coat. Then extrinsic Psycho Paint for mottling, shadowing in the creases (paint darker into the sculpted folds so they read even at rest), and the lips.
- **Paint after the motion test, but within the 2-day window**, or keep a test coupon of the same batch to check adhesion later; keep the paint thin over hinge lines, since every layer adds stiffness.
- **Eyelid edges:** finish the lid skin with a thin rolled edge glued to the printed lid (no free edge to curl), and tint the waterline pinker.
- **Mouth interior:** the brief's mouth mechanism has lip plates behind 8 mm of skin; a separate, softer interior lining (gums, inner lip) glued to the plates avoids having the main skin wrap inside, which is where tear paths start.
- **Seams:** the Frankenstein character gives a free pass: put any section seams on the sculpted scars and stitches and on the forehead/hair line.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for professional intrinsic/extrinsic layer recipes (pigment percentages per layer, number of translucency layers) for animatronic faces specifically.
- No sourced technique found for eyelid edge finishing on servo-driven lids beyond "glue the thin lid skin."
