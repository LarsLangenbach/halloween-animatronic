# Expressive robot faces and animatronic heads: history and landmark systems (1990s to October 2026)

Research notes for Frank's face performance engine (orchestrator, animator, continuous-spectrum synthesiser). Gathered 2026-10-30. Every claim carries a link. Where only a search summary, a press release or marketing copy was available, that is said. Where a third-party AI-generated review (themoonlight.io, alphaxiv) or an aggregator was the only source, it is flagged and treated as weak.

## Q1. Kismet, Leonardo and Breazeal's later work (Jibo): affect space, blending of basis postures, drive/emotion/behaviour architecture

### Takeaway
Kismet (MIT, late 1990s, journal write-up 2003) already did what Frank's "continuous spectrum" synthesiser needs: the emotional state is one point moving through a 3-D affect space (arousal, valence, stance), and the face is a weighted interpolation of nine basis postures placed in that space, so blends and intensities come out of the geometry rather than from a list of discrete faces. Leonardo (2002 onward) added learned expression imitation and social learning. Jibo (2014 to 2018) moved Breazeal's approach into a commercial product with an animation SDK and abstract, cartoon-like expressiveness.

### Cited Findings
**Kismet: the affect space and how the face is built from it**
- Paper of record: C. Breazeal, "Emotion and sociable humanoid robots," *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* 59(1–2), 2003, pp. 119–155, DOI 10.1016/S1071-5819(03)00018-1. — [MIT Media Lab record](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/emotion-and-sociable-humanoid-robots-2); [Georgia Tech PDF copy](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~athomaz/papers/Breazeal-ijhcs03.pdf) (returned HTTP 403 to this session, so it was not read in full).
- MIT's Kismet page: "Kismet's facial expressions are generated using an interpolation-based technique over a three dimensional space," whose "three dimensions correspond to arousal, valence, and stance." The same three attributes are used to assess the internal and environmental factors behind the emotional state, and the current affective state is a single point moving through the space. — [MIT CSAIL Kismet: Facial expressions](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/lbr/sociable/facial-expression.html)
- "There are nine basis (or prototype) postures that collectively span this space of emotive expressions." Each prototype moves most facial features to some degree. For example, the valence prototypes mainly drive lip curvature but also move the ears, eyelids, eyebrows and jaw. — [MIT CSAIL Kismet: Facial expressions](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/lbr/sociable/facial-expression.html)
- "A specific location in affect space specifies the relative contributions of the prototype postures in order to produce a net facial expression that faithfully corresponds to the active emotional state". It runs in real time, and the internal dynamics are designed to give smooth trajectories through the space. — [MIT CSAIL Kismet: Facial expressions](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/lbr/sociable/facial-expression.html)
- The face serves four functions at once: emotive expressions, expressive social displays, behavioural responses (for example closing the eyes against a threat) and synchronisation with speech. "The manner in which these four functions are manifest changes dynamically with motivational state." — [MIT CSAIL Kismet: Facial expressions](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/lbr/sociable/facial-expression.html)
- Secondary sources gloss the axes as arousal (how arousing), valence (how desirable) and stance (how approachable). Perceptual "releasers" are tagged with affective values. For example, a threat releaser is tagged high arousal, negative valence, closed stance. — [Georgia Tech CS7633 lecture slides](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~chernova/CS7633/slides/Lecture11.pdf); [Breazeal, Affective interaction between humans and robots (typeset copy)](https://typeset.io/pdf/affective-interaction-between-humans-and-robots-4bsw0bkc0i.pdf)
- A secondary summary gives the tag range as about −1250 to +1250 per axis. Not checked against the full text. — [search summary of Breazeal 2003 / sambuz slide deck](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/robot-emotions-emotions-of-living-creatures-ppt-presentation-664805)
- The 2022 IJSR survey describes Kismet as generating emotions "using an interpolation-based technique over a 3-D space, where the three dimensions correspond to valence, arousal and stance." — [Rawal & Stock-Homburg 2022, arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07169)
- Rodney Brooks (2020) wrote that Disney's 2020 eye control is "better and smoother now than our 1995 implementations on Cog and Kismet". This dates Kismet's gaze/eye work to the mid-1990s. — [IEEE Spectrum, 2 Nov 2020](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)

**Leonardo (MIT Personal Robots Group, body by Stan Winston Studio, completed 2002)**
- 2.5 ft tall social robot, body by Stan Winston Studio, completed 2002. — [Wikipedia: Leonardo (robot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_(robot))
- Described as a "65-degree of freedom (DoF) fully embodied humanoid robot" in Breazeal et al., IJHR 2004. — [Breazeal et al. IJHR 2004 PDF](https://robots.media.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2015/01/Breazeal-etal-IJHR-04.pdf). A spec site gives 69 DOF with 32 in the face. The two counts conflict. — [Wevolver spec](https://www.wevolver.com/specs/leonardo)
- Expression imitation by motor babbling. Leo detects when he is being imitated (contingency detectors) and then trains a neural network that maps the human's facial expression to his own current facial pose. — [MIT OCW MAS.965 reading (2003)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-965-special-topics-in-media-technology-cooperative-machines-fall-2003/70463fbcf582d67115ae20d161fa903d_coopmachread06c.pdf)
- Used for social learning (imitation, tutelage, social referencing) and Theory of Mind for human-robot collaboration. — [Wikipedia: Leonardo (robot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_(robot))

**Jibo (Breazeal's commercial social robot)**
- Unveiled 2014 as a home "social robot". — [IEEE Spectrum, "Cynthia Breazeal unveils Jibo"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cynthia-breazeal-unveils-jibo-a-social-robot-for-the-home)
- The animation system coordinated output across motors, light ring and the single-eye screen graphics. The SDK exposed scripted animation playback plus "expressive look-at and orientation behaviors" so developers could choreograph actions, dialogue, gestures and expressions. The source is an animation-studies blog citing Jibo's (now offline) developer site. — [Animation Studies blog, "Social robots and cartoons"](https://blog.animationstudies.org/social-robots-and-cartoons/)
- Three motors and a belt system, with a head that swivels 360°. Breazeal aimed for "anthropomorphic without pushing too far". — [HeroX blog](https://www.herox.com/blog/297-meet-jibo-the-worlds-first-social-family-robot); [IEEE Spectrum review (2017)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/jibo-is-as-good-as-social-robots-get-but-is-that-good-enough)

### Inferences
- Kismet is the direct ancestor of Frank's synthesiser. The pattern is: state = point in a low-dimensional affect space → weights over a small set of authored "basis poses" → weighted sum per servo. Frank's 10 LLM tags can be placed as anchor points in such a space (for example valence/arousal/stance or similar), so a tag becomes a target point and the synthesiser interpolates. This is roughly 25 years old and well proven for readable expressions.
- Kismet's "four functions share one face, weighted by motivational state" maps directly onto Frank's arbitration problem: emotion, social display (nod, eyebrow flash), reflex (blink/flinch) and speech (jaw/lips) all want the same servos.
- Leonardo's babbling-and-imitation result is the earliest instance of the "learn the self-model" line that Columbia's Eva and Emo later pushed with deep learning.

### Gaps
- The full text of Breazeal 2003 could not be retrieved (403 and 404 on the copies tried). The detailed architecture (emotion elicitors → emotion activation → arbitration, the drive homeostasis model, the exact coordinates of the nine basis postures, update rates and decay constants) is widely described as a winner-take-all emotion arbitration feeding an expression blend, but that was **not verified in this session**.
- Kismet's facial DOF count was not found in a fetched source. It is commonly cited as about 15 facial DOF plus eyes and neck, but this is unverified.
- No primary documentation of Jibo's animation SDK API (it went offline when Jibo Inc. closed). The company's closure and later revival were not researched here.

## Q2. Other academic faces: Hanson (Sophia, Einstein/Zeno), Geminoid/ERICA/Nikola, WE-4RII, iCat, EMYS, Probo, Furhat and KTH gaze, Columbia's Eva and Emo

### Takeaway
Academic faces split into three families. (a) **Emotion-space models** map a continuous internal state to the face: WE-4RII (3-D pleasantness/activation/certainty plus mood and desires), Probo (emotion as a vector in Russell's 2-D circumplex) and Kismet. (b) **FACS/action-unit builds** design actuators per facial action unit and hand-author basic emotions: EMYS, Nikola, the Ishiguro androids and Hanson. (c) **Learned self-models**: Leonardo, then Columbia's Eva (2021), Emo (2024) and Emo's lip sync (2026), where the robot learns motor→appearance by watching itself. The animation-engine idea (layer gaze, blink, lip sync and expressions concurrently) is clearly stated in Philips' iCat work (2004).

### Cited Findings
**Waseda WE-4RII (Takanishi/Miwa, about 2004)**
- Defines a "3D Mental Space" with certainty, activation and pleasantness axes and a "mental vector" with an emotional trajectory over time. A "Sensing Personality" determines how a sensed stimulus moves the mental state. — [Takanishi, Humanoids 2004 slides (KIT archive)](https://archive.iar.kit.edu/workshops/humanoids2004/Takanishi.pdf)
- Emotion has three components (pleasantness, arousal, certainty), mood has two (pleasantness, arousal), and there are separate desires (appetite, safety, exploration). — [Waseda doctoral thesis (Japanese)](https://waseda.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21255/files/Honbun-4511_10.pdf)
- 59 DOF in total, 26 of them for facial expression. Expresses seven emotions (Ekman's six plus neutral) through face, facial colour and body. — [PMC: Brain response to a humanoid robot (uses WE-4RII)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2908128/)

**Philips iCat (van Breemen, 2004 to 2006)**
- A. J. N. van Breemen, "Animation engine for believable interactive user-interface robots," IROS 2004, pp. 2873–2878. — [DBLP](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/77/6723.html)
- The abstract-level description: the engine combines interactive behaviours with believable animation. The example is iCat speaking to a user while tracking their head, lip-syncing, blinking and showing expressions, all at once. — [Synapse Social listing (garbled, approximate)](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2101506210)
- Follow-up: "Advanced Animation Engine for User-Interface Robots," IROS 2006 (van Breemen & Xue). The iCat animation tools came from the Open Platform for Personal Robots. — [DBLP IROS 2006](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/iros/BreemenX06.html); [AI & Society 2009 iCat gaze paper](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/doi/10.1007/s00146-009-0198-1)
- iCat as a home companion, from Philips Research. — [ERCIM News 67](https://ercim-news.ercim.eu/en67/special-theme-embedded-intelligence/icat-a-friendly-robot-that-helps-children-and-grown-ups)

**Probo (VUB Brussels, Saldien et al., 2008 to 2010)**
- Basic expressions are vectors in a 2-D emotion space derived from Russell's circumplex of affect. The head is fully actuated with 20 DOF, for expressions and eye contact. — [Saldien et al., IJSR 2010 (via Unpaywall)](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS12369-010-0067-6); [typeset copy "Expressing emotions with the social robot Probo"](https://typeset.io/pdf/expressing-emotions-with-the-social-robot-probo-26xsv3ejcs.pdf)
- An "emotional interface" maps the emotion vector to the robot's DOFs. A defined set of action units expresses basic emotions, and a 3-D virtual replica was used to tune and evaluate expressions. — [U. Antwerp repository](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/link/irua/200707); [On the design of the huggable robot Probo](https://rua.ua.es/dspace/handle/10045/12583)

**EMYS (Wrocław, 2013)**
- Kędzierski et al., "EMYS—Emotive Head of a Social Robot," IJSR 5(2):237–249, 2013. Motion control is based on FACS, six basic emotions were designed, and they were tested with children aged 8–12. Built in the EU FP7 LIREC project. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12369-013-0183-1.pdf); [Peeref abstract](https://peeref.com/works/17019671)

**Ishiguro androids (Geminoid, ERICA) and RIKEN's Nikola**
- Geminoid HI-1 (2006), modelled on Hiroshi Ishiguro, is teleoperated. A motion-capture system on the operator drives the lips and head, and the android speaks with the operator's voice. — [Engadget tag page](https://www.engadget.com/tag/GeminoidHi-1); [Sunday Observer 2006](https://archives.sundayobserver.lk/2006/08/20/jun05.asp)
- Later work generates lip motion automatically from the operator's voice formants, tested on Geminoid-F and Telenoid-R2. — [Ishi et al., Interspeech 2012](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2012/ishi12_interspeech.html); [CiNii record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390282679706381568?lang=en)
- ERICA is a platform for *autonomous* conversation with voice, gesture, facial expression, eye contact and touch. It was upgraded with "attentive listening" backchannels (nods, short acknowledgements). — [Osaka Univ. Ishiguro Lab ERICA page](https://eng.irl.sys.es.osaka-u.ac.jp/projects/erica); [Electronic Specifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=73681)
- ERICA served as an AI interviewer at SIGDIAL 2024: 42 participants, 69 % positive. The authors note a need for more adaptive, LLM-driven follow-ups, and some participants were uncomfortable with the human-like look. — [COLING 2025 demo paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-demos.14.pdf)
- Nikola (RIKEN, 2022): 29 pneumatic actuators for the face plus 6 for head and eyeballs, placed according to FACS. 17 action units were produced correctly. Silicone skin wrinkles poorly, so disgust (nose wrinkling) was hard to show. — [RIKEN press release 2022-02-10](https://www.riken.jp/en/news_pubs/research_news/pr/2022/20220210_1/index.html); [Sato et al., Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.800657/pdf)
- Nikola with an LLM (Dec 2025): GPT-4o receives the user's described situation and the full label set and returns three **discrete** labels from a bank of 63 hand-designed expressions (127 labels, each at low or high intensity). "The system was designed to regenerate entire facial expressions at the level of emotion labels" and cannot adjust individual AUs. Abstract instructions gave higher "appropriate response" (p = 0.047) and "emotional expression" (p = 0.026) ratings, with small effects. Only 327 of 624 sessions were "smooth"; retries and errors lowered the scores. — [Fujii et al., Frontiers in Robotics and AI 12 (2025), doi 10.3389/frobt.2025.1728647](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2025.1728647/full)

**Hanson Robotics (Sophia)**
- Sophia's face uses Hanson's "Frubber" skin, which is said to simulate the major 48 muscles of the human face. The face is driven by facial animation authored in Blender and run through ROS. This comes from Hanson materials and a related arXiv paper and was not independently verified. — [Hanson Robotics blog: Sophia 2020](https://hansonrobotics.com/?p=80018); [arXiv 2011.10363 (Sophia 2020 platform)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.10363.pdf)
- Per a search snippet of the full text of *Sophia-in-Audition* (2024), Hanson provides a ROS SDK with three control methods, including a "performance API" that orchestrates pre-created animations and expressions. The abstract itself only mentions "tools for sophisticated performance control" and facial motion-transfer algorithms. — [arXiv 2402.06978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06978)
- Hanson blog: a transformer maps Apple ARKit face parameters to Sophia's motor parameters for performance-driven animation. A 2024 ShanghaiTech course project notes Sophia's control "relies heavily on artist-created, predefined expressions". — [Hanson Robotics blog](https://hansonrobotics.com/?p=79493); [ShanghaiTech report](https://robotics.shanghaitech.edu.cn/sites/default/data/2025-04/robotcs2024project-sophia.pdf)

**Furhat (KTH Stockholm, 2012, later Furhat Robotics)**
- Al Moubayed, Beskow, Skantze & Granström, "Furhat: a back-projected human-like robot head for multiparty human-machine interaction" (LNCS 7403, 2012). A computer-animated face is projected onto a translucent printed mask on a 2-DOF pan-tilt neck. The authors argue a 3-D face avoids the "Mona Lisa effect" of flat screens, where a face seems to look at everyone at once, and so conveys gaze direction accurately. — [Springer chapter](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-34584-5_9); [typeset PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/furhat-a-back-projected-human-like-robot-head-for-multiparty-2tnwk4twds.pdf)
- Gaze-perception studies measured how accurately people read Furhat's gaze by eye design, head movement and viewing angle. An 18-subject study compared synthesised and human gaze from frontal and 45° views. Furhat interacted with thousands of museum visitors at London's Science Museum. — [Kalin Stefanov PDF (gaze study)](https://kalin-stefanov.github.io/assets/pdf/2388676.2388736.pdf); [KTH news](https://www.kth.se/en/aktuellt/nyheter/social-robot-faces-the-real-world-1.437763)
- Furhat Remote API: a custom gesture is a list of timed frames, each a `params` map of facial parameters (for example `BLINK_LEFT`), sent with a class name. Named built-in gestures are triggered by name. — [furhat-remote-api on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/furhat-remote-api/0.3/); [FurhatJSCore](https://github.com/FurhatRobotics/FurhatJSCore/blob/master/)
- Community LLM layer "expressive-furhat": a multi-stage LLM pipeline (OpenAI or Ollama) detects mood changes and generates matching facial expressions and head movements, as a drop-in replacement for the remote API. — [expressive-furhat on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/expressive-furhat/)
- 2025 Frontiers paper: small language models generate text and gestures together, tested on Furhat (face) and Pepper (body). — [Frontiers in Robotics and AI, doi 10.3389/frobt.2025.1581024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2025.1581024/pdf)

**Columbia Creative Machines Lab (Hod Lipson): Eva (2021), Emo (2024), Emo lip sync (2026)**
- Eva: Chen, Hu, Li, Cummings & Lipson, "Smile Like You Mean It: Driving Animatronic Robotic Face with Learned Models," ICRA 2021. Cable-and-motor artificial "muscles" pull points on the face. Learning has two stages: networks link motor commands to video of Eva's own face, then a second network maps a human face from the camera onto that self-image. Blueprints are open source (HardwareX, April 2021). The authors call mimicry "a far cry from the complex ways in which humans communicate." — [Columbia press release "The Robot Smiled Back"](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/press-release/the-robot-smiled-back); [project page](http://www.cs.columbia.edu/~bchen/aiface/)
- Emo: Hu, Chen, Lin, Wang, Wang, Mehlman & Lipson, "Human-robot facial coexpression," *Science Robotics* 9(88), March 2024, DOI 10.1126/scirobotics.adi4724. It has 26 actuators under soft silicone skin and high-resolution cameras in each eye. It learned its self-model from hours of random motor babbling in front of a camera. It predicts a forthcoming human smile about 840 ms ahead and smiles *with* the person. The rationale: "delayed facial mimicry looks disingenuous, but facial co-expression feels more genuine." Next step stated in 2024: add LLM-driven speech. — [Columbia Engineering news](https://engineering.columbia.edu/news/robot-can-you-say-cheese); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1039361)
- Emo lip sync: "Learning Realistic Lip Motions for Humanoid Face Robots," *Science Robotics*, January 2026 (cover). Emo first learned from a mirror, then from videos of humans talking and singing, with a vision-to-action model linking face motion to sound. It works across languages and in song, but "We had particular difficulties with hard sounds like 'B' and with sounds involving lip puckering, such as 'W'." — [Columbia Engineering news, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/robotics/lip-syncing-robot/)

**Surveys**
- Rawal & Stock-Homburg, "Facial Emotion Expressions in Human–Robot Interaction: A Survey," IJSR 14:1583–1604 (2022), covering 2000–2020. It divides robot expression generation into **hand-coded** and **automated** approaches. Open access. — [TU Darmstadt repository](https://tuprints.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/handle/tuda/12608); [arXiv 2103.07169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07169)

### Inferences
- Frank's servo set (2 eye axes, 4 lids, 4 brow ends, jaw, lips, mouth corners) resembles an action-unit build like EMYS or Probo more than a full android: each servo maps to one or two AUs (brow inner raise ≈ AU1, outer raise ≈ AU2, lip corner pull ≈ AU12, jaw drop ≈ AU26). A FACS-style intermediate layer (emotion → AU intensities → servo angles) is well precedented and makes the poses retargetable.
- The Nikola 2025 result is a caution for Frank: an LLM choosing whole discrete expressions from a bank works, but it "cannot adjust individual action units" and retries hurt ratings. Frank's plan (LLM tags → continuous synthesiser) sits one level above Nikola's design and is closer to Kismet's.
- Furhat's per-parameter, timed-frame gesture format is a good, simple model for Frank's "emotion animator" clips (nod, brow flash, blink): keyframes per named channel, layered on the base expression.
- Emo's 840 ms anticipation shows that *timing* (expression arriving with or slightly before the speech beat, not after) matters as much as shape. That argues for the orchestrator scheduling expression changes ahead of TTS audio.

### Gaps
- Hanson's Einstein/Zeno heads and the UCSD MPLab work on learning facial expressions for the Einstein robot (Wu, Butko, Ruvolo, Bartlett, Movellan, about 2009) were not researched in this session. That work is a likely precursor of Emo-style self-modelling and is worth a follow-up.
- No primary Hanson paper describing Sophia's runtime expression architecture (blending, arbitration) was read. The details above come from blog and press material.
- KTH turn-taking gaze research (Skantze et al.) beyond the 2012 Furhat papers was not covered. The official Furhat docs (docs.furhat.io) were not fetched, so `BasicParams` names and any built-in blending semantics are unverified.
- The full EMYS and Probo papers were not read. The exact emotion coordinates and recognition rates are missing.
- The 2006 Geminoid HI-1 DOF counts and ERICA's current LLM integration are not documented in the sources found.

## Q3. Industry: Engineered Arts (Ameca, Mesmer, Tritium), Disney (Audio-Animatronics, A-1000, Shaman, Stuntronics, gaze 2020, BDX, Olaf), Anki Cozmo/Vector, Jibo, Moxie, Apple ELEGNT, recent entrants

### Takeaway
Industry systems are *animation-first*. Artists author poses and clips (Maya, Tritium Animator), a runtime engine layers an always-on idle/"human behaviour" layer (blink, breathing, saccades, look-around) under triggered clips and live puppeteering or AI-driven selection, and the low-level controller only executes. Disney's 2024–2025 papers (the bipedal droid and Olaf) publish this layering explicitly, with blend times and update rates. Engineered Arts publishes only marketing-level detail but describes the same layers (Poses, Animator, autonomous Human Behaviours, an AI layer that picks expressions). LLMs choosing expressions date from Ameca's March 2023 GPT-3 demo.

**Disney Research: "Realistic and Interactive Robot Gaze" (IROS 2020)**
- Pan, Choi, Kennedy, McIntosh, Zamora, Niemeyer, Kim, Wieland & Christensen (Disney Research, Caltech, UIUC, WDI). Built on a WDI Audio-Animatronics bust with neck, eyes, eyelids and eyebrows. — [bibbase record](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/pan-choi-kennedy-mcintosh-zamora-niemeyer-kim-wieland-etal-realisticandinteractiverobotgaze-2020); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)
- An RGB-D camera finds people. Each person gets a "curiosity score", "currently simplified to be based on how much motion it sees", and the top-scoring person drives the state logic. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)
- Behaviour states: **Read** (default, looking at a book), **Glance** (curiosity over threshold), **Engage** (looks with "both the eyes and head") and **Acknowledge** (person judged familiar). Underneath run breathing, small head movements, blinking and saccades in a subsumption architecture: "higher behavioral levels are able to subsume lower behaviors." — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)
- Attention habituation: a sudden sound can briefly pull the gaze before it returns to the face. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/realistic-and-interactive-robot-gaze-by-disney-research/)
- The design stance is "more animation-y than biology-y or psychology-y." The authors say the architecture "lends itself as a framework for organizing animatronic behaviors." — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)

**Disney Research: bipedal robotic character (RSS 2024 / arXiv Jan 2025), the robot shown as the "BDX" droids**
- Grandia, Knoop, Hopkins, Wiedebach, Bishop, Pickles, Müller & Bächer, "Design and Control of a Bipedal Robotic Character," arXiv 2501.05204, listed as RSS 2024. An RL control architecture executes artistic motions from command signals that an animation engine produces by blending multiple animation sources. An operator interface supports live shows. — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05204); [Disney Research page](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/design-and-control-of-a-bipedal-robotic-character/)
- The animation engine (from the full paper) has four sources. **Background**: a looped idle that "conveys a basic level of activity that includes intermittent eye-blinking and antenna motion". **Triggered**: artist clips mapped to buttons. **Joystick**: live offsets. **Episodic**: run-to-completion moves. — [arXiv 2501.05204 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)
- Blending: triggered clips blend over the background with a show-function ratio β and a body ratio α, both ramped linearly (slerp for orientation), over **0.1 s for β (show functions such as eyes and antennas) and 0.35 s for α (body)**, so facial/show elements change faster than the body. The joystick layer is applied **last**, as a non-linear mapping of the blended state. — [arXiv 2501.05204 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)
- Show functions (antennas, eye colour, eye radii, head lamp) are "controlled in an open-loop fashion" and kept out of the RL policy. The neck and head pose are part of the policy command. Show functions react to state: at top speed "the antennas duck back and the eye radii narrow to convey exertion." — [arXiv 2501.05204 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)
- Rates: the policy runs at 50 Hz and the actuators at 600 Hz, bridged by a "first-order-hold, i.e. a linear interpolation of the previous and current policy action" and a 37.5 Hz low-pass filter. — [arXiv 2501.05204 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)
- Operator: a Steam Deck-style controller. The left stick sets posture and the right stick sets gaze. Gaze uses the head first, and the torso adds motion when the neck hits its limits. Buttons trigger emotional clips. Audio clips can be triggered at any time, can be synced to animations, and some sounds are modulated by actuator speed. Episodic motions are authored in Maya. — [arXiv 2501.05204 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204)

**Disney Research: Olaf (arXiv 2512.16705, Dec 2025)**
- "Olaf: Bringing an Animated Character to Life in the Physical World." Standing and walking use RL policies (PPO), with rewards for animation tracking, balance, physical limits, thermal awareness and footstep-noise reduction. — [WDWMAGIC summary, 19 Dec 2025](https://www.wdwmagic.com/other/walt-disney-imagineering/news/19dec2025-inside-disneys-self-walking-olaf-new-research-details-how-the-character-works.htm); [Disney Research PDF](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Olaf-Website-1.pdf)
- Show functions (eyes, eyelids, eyebrows, jaw, arms) use classical control, because their low inertia barely affects the dynamics. Each eye actuator uses a first-order (linear) functional-to-actuator map. Eye pitch and lid are driven through 4-bar linkages. A single jaw actuator gets feedforward torque c₀ + c₁q + c_cos·cos(q) to fight costume forces, and "all eye and arm actuators are controlled using a PD loop." — [arXiv 2512.16705 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)
- The Animation Engine has three stages. **Background** "plays looped whole-body animations to introduce subtle idle behaviors such as eye saccades and arm adjustments." **Triggered** clips (gestures, spoken lines) are layered on top. **Joystick** "adjusts the output pose based on teleoperation inputs." Gaze is joystick-driven, and "Olaf is puppeteered via a remote interface." — [arXiv 2512.16705 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)

### Inferences
- Disney's published engine maps closely onto Frank's needs, and with hobby servos Frank is in the "show function" regime (low inertia, open-loop position control), so no RL is needed. The transferable parts are: (1) an always-on looped background layer carrying blinks and saccades; (2) triggered clips blended over it with short linear ramps (about 0.1 s for face elements); (3) a live input layer (for Frank, the webcam gaze target) applied last; (4) a policy or servo loop at 50 Hz with interpolation to the actuator rate. Frank's 25–50 Hz laptop→Pico link is in the same range as Disney's 50 Hz policy rate.
- Disney's gaze paper gives Frank's gaze subsystem a ready template: a curiosity/attention score per tracked face, a small state machine (idle/read, glance, engage, acknowledge) and subsumed low-level layers (saccades, blinks, breathing micro-motion).
- Engineered Arts' public description (Poses + Animator + autonomous Human Behaviours + AI picking expressions) is the same three-tier split. Its 2023 lesson (GPT-4 too slow, so "thinking" behaviour is authored) means Frank needs an authored "listening/thinking" state that plays while the LLM is busy.
- Anki's "stable emotions, no rapid flipping" and per-behaviour cool-downs are a practical spec for Frank's orchestrator: hysteresis and minimum dwell time on emotion changes.

- Engineered Arts' Tritium and Virtual Robot technical documentation (blend model, how AI output reaches the animation layer, lip-sync method) could not be read. Mesmer (Engineered Arts' realistic-human line based on 3-D scans) is only described in aggregator material here, so its dates and specs are unverified.

- Anki's GDC slides and the Cozmo/Vector mood-manager source (the emotion dimensions themselves) were not retrieved. The community study does not give the dimensions.

## Q4. How they architect expression: discrete poses vs blended spaces, layering of idle/gaze/blink/speech, who decides (AI vs motor controller)

### Takeaway
There are three generations. (1) **Continuous affect space → weighted basis poses** (Kismet; WE-4RII's 3-D mental vector; Probo's circumplex vector). (2) **Authored discrete library + layered animation engine** (iCat, Cozmo/Vector, Ameca's 50+ expressions, Disney's background/triggered/puppeteer stack, Nikola's 63-expression bank). (3) **Learned mappings** (Eva and Emo self-models, Sophia's ARKit transformer) and **LLM selection or generation** (Ameca 2023, Xpress 2025, Nikola 2025, SeM² 2026). In every published system the AI or behaviour layer decides *what* (a label, intensity or target), and a separate animation/motor layer decides *how* (blending, timing, limits). No published system lets an LLM write servo angles directly in real time.

### Cited Findings
- **Continuous blend (Kismet):** a location in affect space sets the relative contributions of nine prototype postures to the net expression, in real time, with smooth trajectories. — [MIT CSAIL Kismet](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/lbr/sociable/facial-expression.html)
- **Continuous mental vector plus mood plus desires (WE-4RII):** emotion is 3-D (pleasantness, activation/arousal, certainty) and mood 2-D. — [Takanishi 2004 slides](https://archive.iar.kit.edu/workshops/humanoids2004/Takanishi.pdf); [Waseda thesis](https://waseda.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/21255/files/Honbun-4511_10.pdf)
- **Circumplex vector (Probo):** emotion as a 2-D vector mapped to 20 head DOF. — [Saldien et al. 2010](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS12369-010-0067-6)
- **Survey taxonomy:** robot expression generation is either hand-coded or automated. — [Rawal & Stock-Homburg 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07169)
- **Concurrent layering (iCat 2004):** speech plus head tracking plus lip sync plus blinking plus expressions run at once through one animation engine. — [Synapse Social listing](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W2101506210)
- **Subsumption layering (Disney gaze 2020):** state machine (Read/Glance/Engage/Acknowledge) over always-on breathing, head micro-motion, blinks and saccades. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/disney-research-robotic-gaze)
- **Animation-engine layering (Disney 2024–2025):** background loop (blinks, saccades) → triggered clips blended in (β 0.1 s for show functions, α 0.35 s for body) → joystick applied last → RL policy at 50 Hz → 600 Hz actuators. Show functions are open-loop and outside the policy. — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204); [arXiv 2512.16705](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)
- **Who decides at Disney:** a human puppeteer triggers clips and steers gaze through the Steam Deck and remote interface. The engine composes, the policy executes. — [arXiv 2501.05204](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.05204); [arXiv 2512.16705](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16705)
- **Who decides at Engineered Arts:** an LLM (GPT-3, 2023) *selects* expressions from the authored set, and the passive "Human Behaviours" layer (blink, breathing, eye contact, look-around) runs autonomously. — [Lifeboat 2023](https://lifeboat.com/blog/2023/04/researchers-train-worlds-most-advanced-humanoid-robot-ameca-on-gpt-4-finds-her-less-responsive); [engineeredarts.com/robots/ameca](https://engineeredarts.com/robots/ameca)
- **LLM-generated expressions (Xpress, JHU, March 2025):** a three-phase GPT-4 pipeline. (1) TTS and STT timestamps are segmented by narrative or emotional shifts. (2) A second GPT-4 writes natural-language face descriptions conditioned on prior segments. (3) GPT-4 turns each description into JavaScript face code (generate, verify, refine), giving a timestamped trajectory on a 28-DoF *screen* face. It takes about 7 minutes per 500-word story, so it is pre-generated. In conversation it switches to a pre-generated bank of 13 emotions × 2 intensities: a GPT-4o-mini "Reactor" picks a reaction every 5-word chunk while the user speaks, and a GPT-4o "Responder" picks the emotion and intensity for its reply. The expression shows for the first 3 s of speech and then resets to neutral. Code was 100 % executable, context match 71.67 % (stories) and 75.00 % (conversation). Limitations: text-only, latency, intensity mismatches, timing that was sometimes laggy or "a bit too timely". — [arXiv 2503.00283 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00283v1)
- **LLM selects discrete labels (Nikola, Dec 2025):** GPT-4o returns three labels from 63 authored expressions, whole expressions are swapped at the label level, with no AU-level control. — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2025.1728647/full)
- **VLM plus temporal alignment (SeM², Feb 2026):** multimodal perception → chain-of-thought response planning → a "Semantic-Sequence Aligning Mechanism" that aligns verbal content and physical expressions in time. An edge-distilled version keeps 95 % of the relative performance. The robot platform and output format are not given in the abstract. — [arXiv 2602.07434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07434)
- **Learned motor mapping (Eva 2021, Emo 2024, Emo lip sync 2026):** the inverse model from desired appearance to motor commands is learned from self-observation, so poses do not need hand-authoring. — [Columbia 2021](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/press-release/the-robot-smiled-back); [Columbia 2024](https://engineering.columbia.edu/news/robot-can-you-say-cheese); [Columbia 2026](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync)
- **Performance retargeting (Sophia):** a transformer maps ARKit face parameters to motor parameters. — [Hanson Robotics blog](https://hansonrobotics.com/?p=79493)

### Inferences
- Frank's planned split (LLM tags → orchestrator → animator → synthesiser → servo angles at 25–50 Hz) matches the consensus architecture: a slow, sparse decision layer (LLM, about 1 decision per utterance or phrase) above a fast continuous layer (blending, idle, gaze, lip sync) that never waits on the LLM.
- Frank's "continuous spectrum" synthesiser is a Kismet-style design, not a library-swap design like Nikola or Xpress-conversation. Its advantage over the 2023–2026 LLM systems is that intensity and blends are native (move the point, don't swap the face), which addresses the intensity mismatch and the reset-to-neutral artefacts Xpress reports.
- A concrete priority order consistent with Kismet and Disney is: reflexes/blinks > speech mouth (jaw and lips) > emotion expression on the mouth corners and brows > idle micro-motion, with gaze as a separate channel and the webcam target applied last, as Disney applies the joystick last.
- Blend-time asymmetry (Disney's 0.1 s for face elements, 0.35 s for body) suggests short ramps for brows and lids and longer, eased transitions for the emotional "point" itself, plus dwell and hysteresis per Anki.

### Gaps
- No public source describes how Engineered Arts blends an LLM-chosen expression with ongoing speech visemes and idle (additive vs override, ramp times).
- No published system was found that drives a *physical* servo face from a *continuous* LLM-emitted affect vector, as opposed to discrete labels or pre-generated code. That suggests Frank's design is novel in combination, but the absence may only be a gap in this search.
- Lip-sync methods in commercial heads (viseme tables vs audio-amplitude jaw vs learned) are unpublished for Ameca, Sophia and Disney.

## Q5. How new is this? What is decades old, what is recent (LLM-driven, 2023–2026), what is still open, with a timeline

### Takeaway
Continuous affect-space expression, basis-pose blending, layered idle/gaze/blink/speech engines and gaze state machines are 20–30 years old and well understood (Kismet mid-1990s to 2003, WE-4RII 2004, iCat 2004, Probo 2010, Disney gaze 2020). What is new since 2021 is learned self-models of the face (Eva, Emo, lip sync) and, since 2023, language models choosing or generating expressions (Ameca, Xpress, Nikola, SeM²). The open problems are real-time latency, matching intensity to context, keeping expression continuous and well-timed rather than swapped labels, lip sync for plosives and rounded vowels, evaluation, and the uncanny valley.

**Open problems stated by the sources**
- Latency: GPT-4 made Ameca "less responsive" (2023). Xpress needs about 7 min of pre-generation per 500 words and suggests "smaller language models could enable real-time generation." — [Lifeboat](https://lifeboat.com/blog/2023/04/researchers-train-worlds-most-advanced-humanoid-robot-ameca-on-gpt-4-finds-her-less-responsive); [Xpress](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00283v1)
- Intensity and timing: Xpress reports intensity mismatches, lag and timing that felt "a bit too timely", along with limited emotional depth in conversation. — [Xpress](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00283v1)
- Granularity: Nikola's label-level regeneration "cannot adjust individual action units". — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2025.1728647/full)
- Lip sync: "particular difficulties with hard sounds like 'B'" and puckering sounds like "W" (Emo, 2026). — [Columbia 2026](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/robot-learns-lip-sync)
- Skin limits: silicone doesn't wrinkle well, so disgust (nose wrinkle) is hard to show. — [RIKEN 2022](https://www.riken.jp/en/news_pubs/research_news/pr/2022/20220210_1/index.html)
- Audience: older users were less receptive to expressive motion (ELEGNT), and some participants were uncomfortable with human-like androids (ERICA). — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/02/06/apple-is-working-on-a-lifelike-robot-lamp-and-its-just-as-cute-as-youd-expect); [COLING 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-demos.14.pdf)
- Depth: mimicry "is still a far cry from the complex ways in which humans communicate" (Columbia, 2021). — [Columbia 2021](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/press-release/the-robot-smiled-back)
- Fragility of cloud-dependent character: Moxie's personality died with its servers. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/12/10/moxie-kids-robot-shuts-down)

### Inferences
- **Decades old and safe to copy:** the affect space plus basis-pose interpolation (Kismet), the separate mood/emotion/drive timescales (WE-4RII), concurrent layering of blink, gaze, lip sync and expression (iCat), gaze state machines with curiosity scores and subsumed micro-motion (Disney 2020), and animator-authored clips over a background loop (Cozmo, Disney).
- **Recent (2023–2026):** LLMs emitting expression labels or code (Ameca 2023 onward, Xpress, Nikola, SeM²), learned facial self-models and lip sync (Columbia 2021–2026), and published RL-plus-animation-engine character stacks (Disney 2024–2025).
- **Where Frank sits:** the combination of an LLM emitting discrete tags, a *continuous* synthesiser that interpolates between anchor poses with intensity, a Disney-style layered animator (idle loop, triggered gestures, gaze last) and a Kismet-style arbitration over a small hobby-servo face is not, as far as these sources show, published as one system. Each piece has strong precedent, so the engineering risk is low. The novelty is in the integration and the timing (expression scheduled against TTS rather than after it, Emo-style).
- Because Frank's LLM is cloud or laptop based, the Moxie and Ameca lessons apply: keep idle, gaze, blink and a "thinking" pose fully local and authored, so Frank stays alive when the LLM is slow or offline.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for an industry system emitting a continuous affect vector from an LLM to a physical face. It may exist in unpublished commercial stacks (Engineered Arts, Hanson).
- The Disney BDX naming, the Audio-Animatronics founding dates and the Kismet DOF and arbitration details still need primary-source confirmation (see Q1 and Q3).
- No 2026 Engineered Arts or Hanson primary updates on expression architecture were found. Their current state (as of October 2026) is known only from undated product pages.
